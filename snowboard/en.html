⚖️ Where is your balance?

Yellow ball = centre of mass (CoM), roughly behind your belly button in the middle of the pelvis. The line drops from the CoM to the snow along your body’s lean; the ring is where it lands (green = right, orange = drifting, red = off). It should be between your feet.

How to find your own balance point (at home / on snow)
  1. Strapped in on flat ground, knees and ankles slightly bent, rock slowly forward and back: lean back and the nose lifts, lean forward and the tail lifts. The steadiest point with both feet about even is your start position.
  2. Close your eyes and rock again, feeling how the pressure moves between your feet.
  3. When gliding straight, bend the front knee and look ahead, not at the board: looking down usually puts your weight on the back foot.
  4. Keep your hands relaxed, slightly in front at your sides; hands drifting behind usually means you are sitting back.
  5. On snow: while gliding, lift the tail and put it down, then lift the nose. Once you can, you know how to move fore and aft.

Toeside vs heelside:Toeside mainly uses the calves to press the toes down and the glutes to keep the hips from pushing back; heelside mainly uses the tibialis anterior to lift the toes, with the quads and abs holding the ’seated’ position. That’s why many people’s thighs and abs tire faster on heelside.

Stance

Switch: your usual back foot becomes the front foot. The muscle and fore/aft pressure pattern is the same as normal but mirrored: your usual back foot’s tibialis anterior and calf now do the front-foot job, so switch usually feels clumsy. A duck stance (+15/−6) becomes +6/−15 in switch; the front foot angle is smaller, so the board responds a little slower.

Board R = sidecut radius. When carving, turn radius ≈ R × cos(edge angle) and lean angle comes from tan θ = v² / (g·r). On a snowboard the edge angle is about equal to body lean, so the carving balance condition simplifies to sin θ ≈ v² / (g·R). A real board can’t bend without limit, so the model keeps the turn radius at 0.6 R or more. Bindings at +15° / −6° (duck), stance width ≈ height × 0.31. Recommended ranges are rules of thumb.
